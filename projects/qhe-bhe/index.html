Research Direction

In Loop Quantum Gravity, spacetime is described by spin networks — graphs whose edges carry quantum geometric information. When we consider a triangulated sphere as a discretization of space, the dimer coverings of its dual graph correspond to quantum states of geometry.

This project investigates whether these quantum geometric states exhibit the same topological quantization as the Quantum Hall Effect. Specifically, we ask: does the Berry phase acquired under flux threading on a triangulated sphere equal the Chern number of the corresponding QHE state?

Our numerics show that the correspondence is geometric, not merely topological. It requires uniform curvature (regular polyhedra) — arbitrary random triangulations destroy the quantization. This aligns with the LQG expectation that triangulation vertices and edges carry geometric information, and that spherical symmetry (vacuum) must be preserved.
